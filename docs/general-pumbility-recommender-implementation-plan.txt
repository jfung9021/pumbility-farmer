2. Current behavior that must change

General currently uses three different player-skill calculations:
  - Candidate skill: mean Top 20 chart Pumbility, converted into the continuous
    chart level that would earn S with Fair Game.
  - Projection skill: mean Pumbility at ranks 11-30, converted into the same
    level units, including leave-one-out adjustments.
  - Clearing skill: average current official level of the hardest 50 unique
    successful clears, calculated separately.

Relevant locations:
  piu_recommendations.py:3076-3173,4214-4705
  player_skill_ratings.py:82

The first two skills also drive floor(skill) +/-2 official-level candidate
windows and the skill+1 estimated-difficulty ceiling. Those bounds cannot accept
a raw Pumbility total and must be removed from the new General path throughout
backend generation, cache projection, local readers, and frontend filtering.
The new General S/D path should not calculate these three skills for its output.
Do not globally replace/remove shared helpers such as _rating_lookup,
_player_mode_scoring_ratings, or clearing_skill_for_chart_ids: other analysis
paths still use them.

3. PIUScores input and baseline contract

Use the existing current Phoenix2 submitted-score snapshot and authorized
individual-player refresh. Build the peer population from available PIUScores
accounts, with no official ID mapping, official ranking, official board fetch,
official missing-score reconstruction, or top-300 gate.

Use a General-specific normalization path before legacy frame cleaning drops
zero values. _clean_snapshot_dataframes currently requires pumbility >0
(piu_recommendations.py:830), whereas a known valid zero-Pumbility result must
remain distinguishable from an unknown result.

Normalize as follows:
  - Match a nonempty PIUScores account ID and chart ID to the current catalog.
  - Keep current Singles and Doubles charts, including levels below 16 when
    constructing histories and Top 50 baselines.
  - Exclude broken records and nonfinite/negative Pumbility; preserve valid zero.
  - Recorded PIUScores chart Pumbility is authoritative for actual results.
  - Retain one deterministic best observation per account/chart, preferring
    chart Pumbility, then raw score, then timestamp. Do not count duplicates.
  - A baseline observation may have known Pumbility without a usable raw score
    or plate. Such a row remains known baseline history but cannot supply a
    paired target observation. Do not fabricate missing values.
  - Normalize finite, exactly integral raw scores in [0,1000000] to integers;
    sanitized snapshots store numbers such as 995000.0. Reject booleans,
    fractional scores, and out-of-range values as peer-score observations.
  - Normalize actual recorded plates with normalize_plate, accepting existing
    full-name and code aliases. Store the normalized plate with its raw score
    and chart identity. Missing/invalid plates are excluded from target pairs.
    Actual-baseline Pumbility remains the submitted value even if plate is absent.

Deduplicate players by existing PIUScores account ID. Do not infer that two
accounts are the same person from their display names or require an official
identity join. Duplicate real-person accounts cannot be resolved by this plan's
account-only inputs.

For each account and each mode independently:
  - Order chart contributions by descending Pumbility cents, then chart ID.
  - Sum the best 50 with decimal/cents arithmetic; preserve two-decimal values.
  - Retain chart contributors, known-result count, cutoff, and source dates.
  - Require at least 50 usable unique observations for mode skill/cohort/gain
    availability. Expose an insufficient-history state at 49; show known Top 50
    history without treating missing entries as zero-valued empty slots.
  - Neither an overall profile total nor the other mode's total replaces this
    mode's rating. Do not convert totals back into difficulty-level units.

Use Phoenix2 only for the new S/D calculation, matching the reference method.
Remove Phoenix1 rating fallback, Phoenix-source weighting, Phoenix1 personal-best
target floors, and personal/population plate projections from this branch.
Existing archive, tier, and Co-op paths retain their own Phoenix1 behavior.

4. Exact peer, target, and gain policy

For a subject with mode Top 50 total P:
  a. Exclude the subject's account. Other accounts need 50 known results in the
     same mode; no overall or opposite-mode skill may substitute.
  b. Try inclusive [P,P+100], then [P,P+150], then [P,P+200]. Stop at the first
     window with at least ten peers and retain ALL peers in that window.
  c. If +200 has fewer than ten, keep those peers and backfill with the nearest
     strictly lower mode totals until ten. Break equal-total backfill ties by
     stable PIUScores account ID; this replaces Pro's official-rank tie-breaker.
  d. If the available population still has fewer than ten, retain its actual
     count and flag limited support. Do not invent peers or pad with self.

These windows are upward-only, not symmetric +/-100. Select one cohort per mode
per subject, not a new cohort for each chart. A chart with no observations in
that cohort is unobserved; missing chart scores cause no further widening.

For each current General-supported mode chart at official level >=16:
  - Take one complete submitted raw-score/actual-plate pair per selected peer
    on that exact chart. Both target statistics use this same paired sample.
    A valid raw score with a missing or invalid plate supplies no target pair.
  - Compute the unweighted linear score median (n-1)*0.5; preserve fractional
    results until grade decisions. No Phoenix-source weighting, response-surface
    fallback, ranks-11-30 restriction, or individual PB floor applies.
  - Compute the ordinal median of those actual plates in this fixed order:
    RG < FG < TG < MG < SG < EG < UG < PG. For an even number of observations,
    choose the lower middle plate (index floor((n-1)/2)); do not interpolate a
    synthetic plate or use the most frequent category instead of the median.
  - No complete pairs: omit the target. One to nine pairs: permit the observed
    medians but mark chart support as limited. Report paired support and the
    count of otherwise usable score observations excluded for missing plates.
  - General no longer derives plate from 995000/1000000 score thresholds. A
    recorded FG/TG/EG/UG median is retained regardless of the score grade.
    Pro keeps its own existing score-to-plate target policy.
  - Truncate the score target to the lower boundary of its earned Phoenix2
    grade; when the selected actual median plate is PG, request 1000000.
  - Reuse phoenix2_pumbility(type,current_level,grade,plate) for expected chart
    Pumbility, including its mode rules and two-decimal truncation.
  - A chart already in the mode Top 50 replaces its own recorded contribution;
    any other chart replaces the known mode 50th cutoff. Retain positive gains.
  - Retain the count of complete peer observations whose raw score is >= the
    displayed target score. Rank the full viable pool using section 8's combined
    gain/count policy. Estimated difficulty is no longer a tie-breaker.

Enumerate eligible charts from the current catalog and attach available tier
annotations. Missing/unrated estimated difficulty must not suppress a chart
with an observed peer target and positive gain. Preserve the level-16 display
floor; do not copy Pro's S24+/D25+ floor or keep a hidden skill-derived ceiling.

For Overall:
  - Construct the strongest combined 50 from known Singles and Doubles facts,
    including known facts from a mode with fewer than 50 results.
  - Require a known combined 50 before estimating overall replacement gains.
  - Opportunities come from ready source modes, preserving their median, grade,
    plate, expected value, and evidence. Recompute only overall replacement gain
    against the combined pool, retaining original mode gain separately.
  - Use a chart's own contribution when already in the combined Top 50, otherwise
    its combined cutoff; retain only positive overall gains.
  - Consolidate complete source lists before the 50-row display cap. One ready
    mode may supply Overall while the other mode is insufficient.

6. Acceptance criteria and bounded verification

Required algorithm cases:
  - PIUScores-only results with no official identity/rank/board dependency;
    changes to official capture data or Phoenix1 inputs do not change new S/D
    ratings, targets, or gains with catalog/display annotations held constant.
  - Exactly 49/50 unique results; duplicate, broken, zero-Pumbility, invalid raw
    score, missing raw score, removed-chart, and below-16 baseline handling.
  - Mode independence; inclusive +100/+150/+200 boundaries; all peers in the
    first successful window; deterministic lower backfill; self exclusion and
    genuinely small populations.
  - No per-chart cohort widening or missing-score zero fill; exact raw medians
    and grade boundaries at 995000, 995000.5, and 1000000. Actual plate medians
    include all eight plate categories, aliases, odd/even sample sizes, lower
    middle ties, missing/invalid plate exclusion, and no complete-pair targets.
    Changing peer plates while holding raw scores constant must affect the
    selected plate, expected Pumbility, and gain when its ordinal median changes.
  - Own-chart versus cutoff replacement, positive-gain filtering, deterministic
    ordering, combined Overall gains, one-mode readiness, and display limits
    applied after consolidation.
  - Equivalent mode totals yield the same pure peer selection as Pro, except
    the account-ID tie-breaker when official ranks differ. Score medians agree
    on the same samples; General's actual-plate target policy is intentionally
    separate. Pro rank500/501 and S23/D24 versus S22/D23 boundaries are covered.
  - A positive-gain S16/D16 candidate and one beyond the old skill window survive
    Python cache projection, API reads, local TS reads, and UI difficulty filters;
    an unrated tier annotation does not silently discard an eligible target.
  - Full build versus persisted-model refresh parity for identical inputs;
    model serialization round-trip; stale-schema rejection; generation switch.
  - Existing Top 50 export fields, Co-op, manual rating, tier calculations, and
    official Pro results retain their existing behavior where touched.

8. Combined gain and target-score support ranking (user amendment)

Apply this ordering to every scored-profile recommendation mode in both views.
Keep each mode's existing viability rules and its full candidate pool. Perform
the ranking after target construction and active-pool gain calculation, before
any display limit. Exact difficulty filters retain those global ranks and order;
they do not rerank a difficulty subset.

Define scoreAtOrAboveTargetCount using actual eligible raw observations:
  - General Singles/Doubles: count complete same-chart score/plate pairs from
    the selected mode cohort whose score is >= targetScore. These are the same
    eligible pairs used by the score and plate target medians.
  - General Overall: preserve each source mode's target-score count and targets,
    recompute gains against the combined Top 50, then rank the full combined
    viable opportunity pool independently.
  - General Co-op: the existing target ladder has no mode peer cohort. Count
    other PIUScores accounts' valid nonbroken Phoenix2 scores on that exact
    chart at or above the selected target, excluding the selected account.
    Store this population support index privately with the immutable model;
    do not introduce Phoenix1 observations into the new support model or change
    Co-op's independent existing goal construction.
  - Pro Singles/Doubles: count raw official scores from the selected mode cohort
    at or above that chart's targetScore. Overall preserves those counts and
    reranks after its combined-pool gains are recalculated.

For a viable list of N charts, use descending competition ranks:
  gainRank(i) = 1 + count(charts with strictly greater projectedGain)
  scoreCountRank(i) = 1 + count(charts with strictly greater
                              scoreAtOrAboveTargetCount)
  rankScore(i) = gainRank(i) + scoreCountRank(i)

Both components have equal weight. Equal metric values share a rank; do not
introduce arbitrary differences between equal support counts or equal gains.
Sort by rankScore ascending, then projectedGain descending, score count
descending, expected chart Pumbility (or Co-op Rating) descending, case-folded
song title, and chart ID. Assign recommendationRank=1..N to that final order.
Expose both component ranks, their sum, final rank, and score count as public
calculation metadata. The rank sum has no Pumbility or chart-level units.

Use the same source-independent Python ranking helper for General, Co-op and
Pro. Update TypeScript readers/validators/filtering to preserve the new order;
they must not silently restore the previous gain-only sort. Include all rank
overrides in compact Overall references, because an Overall chart's component
and final ranks can differ from the same chart's source-mode ranks. Preserve
the explicitly entered manual difficulty route as its existing separate path,
without fabricated peer counts.

Verification cases:
  - A smaller-gain chart with stronger target-score support can outrank a
    higher-gain chart when its combined rank is better.
  - Equality at the target score counts; a score just below does not.
  - Equal gains/counts share competition ranks, sums are correct, and final
    tie-breaking is deterministic and independent of input order.
  - Counts exclude self and use the declared eligible population; General's
    target samples require valid actual plates, while Pro retains its policy.
  - Overall recomputes ranks after gain replacement, compact round-trips retain
    overrides, and filtering or truncating lists cannot change global ranks.
  - Co-op counts survive private model serialization, split-mode merging, full
    builds and individual refreshes, using only Phoenix2 score evidence.
  - All Recommendations and Pro mode readers accept the new method identities
    and reject incompatible prior-order artifacts.
